# Hub: Capitol & City Hall

**Dominant Circle:** Mortalis
**Last updated:** 2026-10-01

---

## Flavor

Richmond's government sits in a handful of buildings within a short walk of each other. Richmond City Hall (900 E Broad St), with its public observation deck, holds the council chamber, the clerk's vault and the long corridors of aides. Capitol Square rolls up the hill around the Virginia State Capitol, and the General Assembly Building is where the Commonwealth's bills are drafted, argued and amended. Beside City Hall squats the gray concrete parking deck that everyone uses and no one looks at.

Mortalis holds this hub by right: it is their work, carried out in public view by people who clock in. Night works the late receptions and the dark galleries, Power works the procedure and the records, and Wild turns up in the forms, the back corridors and the storefronts near the square. Everyone needs a signature from somebody here sooner or later, and so everyone ends up owing somebody.

**Tone:** two shifts on one clock. The day shift is public: numbered tickets, a microphone at the lectern, three minutes per speaker. The night shift starts when the garage gate lifts for the last time, and what gets agreed on the deck's third level is never put in the minutes.

---

## The Overwrite Manifestation

**Overwrite Effect:** Official records change between the morning and afternoon sessions, and only the people who keep the originals notice: a stenographer with her tapes, a records clerk with his true copies.

**Active Anomalies:**
- A motion recorded as failing in the morning reads as passed in the afternoon transcript
- A bill that died in committee reappears on the calendar under its original sponsor's name
- The vault holds deeds dated today in handwriting from a century ago
- A numbered space on the deck's third level is on no permit list, and a car sits in it until sunrise

**MC anchor rule:** reuse the same aide, stenographer, clerk and parking level. A government is a set of people at desks; recurring faces and places make the records feel real before they change.

**MC note:** most people in these buildings have never heard of the Circles: visitors, staff, the public in the gallery. Leave them that way, with no Status and nothing to trade. The fictional brokers here are the exception; they know which Circles they owe, and they sell introductions across that line. Play the clerks as people with jobs, not as pawns, and make a Debt here something a person chooses to take on.

## Hub Moves

### Through the Right Window
When you work the system to get something official done fast (a permit, a record pulled, a hearing moved), roll with Mind.
- **10+:** It's done, and you learn whose desk it crossed.
- **7-9:** It's done, but it costs you a Debt to a clerk, an aide, or a fixer.
- **Miss:** It's stuck, and someone now has a file with your name on it.

### Parking Deck Deal
When you meet someone after dark in the City Hall deck to strike an off-the-books agreement, roll with Mortalis.
- **10+:** The deal holds; both of you get what you came for, and no one saw.
- **7-9:** The deal holds, but choose 1: there's a witness; the terms change before sunrise; you owe a broker for the introduction.
- **Miss:** Someone recorded it, and they want something.

## Adaptation Notes

- Real places (City Hall, Capitol Square, the State Capitol, the General Assembly Building) are named only as settings. Never make a real named official, office-holder or agency supernatural or corrupt; every resident is a fictional person in a fictional role.
- Government workers are people with families, tired feet and principles. Corinne Batiste and Mags Oduya are honest; the Overwrite, not corruption, is the problem to solve.
- Beatrix Alvarenga feeds only by prior arrangement. Keep minors, including school groups on the observation deck, out of any deal, Debt or danger. Virginia's real legislative page program serves young teens: pages stay in the background and out of every deal, Debt and danger.
- Respect player safety limits and keep scenes about power and corruption focused on the fiction.
