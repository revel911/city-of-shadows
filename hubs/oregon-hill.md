# Hub — Oregon Hill

**Hub Type:** Mortalis (Dominant)

**Setting:** Perched on a hill overlooking the James River, Oregon Hill has been a working-class neighborhood since the 1840s when Tredegar Iron Works employees built cottages near the factory. Named by Irish canal workers who joked that moving so far from downtown felt like heading to Oregon Territory. The neighborhood sits wedged between Hollywood Cemetery, VCU campus, and the Downtown Expressway. Despite gentrification pressures, it maintains its blue-collar identity through a tight-knit community of longtime families, artists, and students.

---

## The Overwrite Manifestation

**Overwrite Effect:** Industrial echo — the neighborhood overlays different eras of labor and industry, with phantom work shifts and spectral manufacturing.

**Active Anomalies:**
- Factory whistles blow shift changes for mills that closed decades ago
- Residents wake to the sound of iron forges and smell of coal smoke from the valley below
- Pay stubs appear in mailboxes for work at defunct companies, sometimes with accurate wages for current bills
- Tredegar Iron Works sometimes appears fully operational with workers who don't recognize modern safety equipment
- Houses show foundations and extensions from different periods of construction simultaneously
- Hollywood Cemetery occasionally expands to include graves of people still living in the neighborhood

**Industrial echo strongest at shift-change hours: 6am, 2pm, 10pm.**

---

## Primary Faction

**Mortalis:** Dominant through working-class solidarity and neighborhood pride. Three generations often live within blocks of each other. Residents identify strongly with Oregon Hill's history and resist changes that threaten its character.

**Faction Status:**
- Mortalis: **3** (dominant through community ties and local history)
- Wild: **2** (strong artistic community, musicians, VCU students)
- Power: **1** (VCU expansion pressure, some city planning)

## Hub Moves

### Working Class Solidarity
**Trigger:** When you help a neighbor with practical problems, stand up against outside pressure, or organize collective action.
**Roll:** Heart
- **10+:** The Hill has your back. Choose 2: you solve the immediate problem | you strengthen community bonds | you gain useful information | you establish lasting alliances
- **7-9:** You help, but choose 1 from above and face a complication.
- **Miss:** Good intentions create conflict or draw unwanted attention to the neighborhood.

### Industrial Memory
**Trigger:** When you seek knowledge about the neighborhood's working history or interact with the Overwrite's industrial echoes.
**Roll:** Blood
- **10+:** The work teaches you. Choose 2: you understand exactly what you need to know | you gain insight into how the community functions | phantom labor provides real benefit | you connect with workers across time
- **7-9:** You learn something but choose 1 from above.
- **Miss:** The industrial past is dangerous, and its echoes put you or others at risk.

## Threats & Complications

- **Gentrification Pressure:** VCU expansion and downtown development increase property values, forcing out longtime families. The Overwrite sometimes shows future versions where Oregon Hill has lost its character entirely.
- **Student-Resident Tension:** Large groups of college renters change neighborhood dynamics. Some students unknowingly interact with phantom residents from different eras, causing confusion.
- **Industrial Hazards:** Phantom factory operations create real dangers — toxic fumes from non-existent mills, injuries from spectral machinery, exposure to historical work conditions.
- **Development Conflicts:** City planning sometimes references infrastructure or zoning from multiple time periods simultaneously, creating legal and practical complications.

## Fragments & Beats

- Eddie McAllister tells stories about his grandfather's work at Tredegar, sometimes speaking as if the old man is still showing up for shifts at the restored historical site.
- Lucia's art gallery holds opening nights where attendees include longtime residents who moved away decades ago, returning for one evening to see how the neighborhood has changed.
- Pete Kowalski patrols streets that sometimes show different house numbers or include buildings that were demolished years ago, adjusting his rounds based on which version of the neighborhood he's experiencing.
- St. Andrew's Church holds services where hymns echo with harmonies from iron workers singing during their shifts, creating layered music that spans generations.
- The Community House distributes food and services to families whose needs span multiple decades of neighborhood history, with volunteers serving clients they don't recognize but somehow know.
- Artists working in home studios create pieces influenced by sounds of phantom factory work, incorporating industrial rhythms that haven't been heard in the valley for over a century.
- Hollywood Cemetery visitors sometimes encounter Oregon Hill residents tending graves of family members who are still alive and living blocks away.
- The Expressway occasionally shows alternative routes or doesn't exist at all, allowing foot traffic to flow between Oregon Hill and downtown as it did before the highway divided the community.
